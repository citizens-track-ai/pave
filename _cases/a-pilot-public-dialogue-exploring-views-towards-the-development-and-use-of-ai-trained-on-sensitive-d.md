# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "There are many public benefits associated\
  \ with the development and\nuse of AI trained on sensitive data in Trusted Research\
  \ Environments\n(TREs). AI models can be much more efficient at analysing very large\n\
  datasets than humans, and can be more accurate at drawing\nconclusions from complex\
  \ data.\n\nHowever, AI models trained on sensitive data also present new\nchallenges\
  \ for data privacy. They generally contain a huge amount\nof data, making it difficult\
  \ for humans to assess whether a model is\ndisclosive of the privacy of individuals\
  \ represented in the data they\nwere trained on. They may also be susceptible to\
  \ hacking \u2013\nor privacy attacks \u2013 to uncover sensitive information, and\
  \ there\nare risks of biases and inaccuracies as a result of unrepresentative\n\
  training data.\n\nEffective governance and regulation are key to mitigating the\n\
  potential risks to individuals and society from the development and\nuse of AI trained\
  \ on sensitive data. It is essential that the public, who\nmay be individually or\
  \ collectively impacted by these risks, are\ninvolved in defining what this looks\
  \ like.\n\nThis pilot public dialogue aimed to explore how to effectively engage\n\
  people without existing knowledge and awareness of the subject\nmatter in conversations\
  \ about AI in sensitive data research, and\nuncover their initial views on the topic.\
  \ Three in-person workshops\nwere held with 22 participants in autumn 2025 in Balsall\
  \ Heath, an\narea in Birmingham, England. A mixture of presentations, small group\n\
  discussions and interactive activities enabled participants to develop\ntheir understanding\
  \ of the subject matter and offer informed views.\nParticipants expressed optimism\
  \ about the public benefits of AI\ntrained on sensitive data, but voiced a desire\
  \ for rigorous data privacy\nprocesses, representative training data and clearer\
  \ lines of consent for\ndata use. Trust was central to discussions, with concern\
  \ raised about\ncommercial involvement. There was a clear demand that processes\
  \ to\nprotect sensitive data \u2013 including The Five Safes and regulatory\nrequirements\
  \ \u2013 remain fit for purpose in light of the unique challenges\nposed by AI.\n\
  \nA key challenge throughout the dialogue was maintaining a clear\nfocus on AI trained\
  \ on sensitive data, with conversations frequently\ndrifting towards internet-based\
  \ AI such as ChatGPT. This was likely\ndriven by the significant media discourse\
  \ around mainstream\ninternet-based AI models at the time the workshops took place,\
  \ and\nfuture work will require concerted efforts to maintain a focus on AI in\n\
  the context of sensitive data research.\n\nDARE UK will be taking the learnings\
  \ of this work forward to inform a\nlarger dialogue on the same topic with people\
  \ from across all four\nnations of the UK. The aim will be to set out more specific,\
  \ actionable\nguidance for the development and use of AI trained on sensitive data\n\
  in line with public expectations.\n"

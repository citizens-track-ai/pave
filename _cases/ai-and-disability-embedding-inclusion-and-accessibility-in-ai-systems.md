# Headline findings
headline-findings: '1. Persons with disabilities in Nigeria are already actively using
  AI tools: visual impairment communities reported using Be My Eyes and AI-powered
  vision applications to access printed materials and educational content.

  2. Persons with hearing impairments reported using transcription technologies to
  participate in conversations, and generative AI tools such as ChatGPT for academic
  research and information access.

  3. Participants identified the high cost of assistive technologies as a major barrier,
  calling for subsidised access to AI-enabled tools for persons with disabilities
  and reduced administrative and financial burden on caregivers.

  4. Participants flagged that some navigation and mapping AI systems fail to account
  for disability-specific environmental risks, particularly given Nigeria''s road
  infrastructure.

  5. Participants noted that some AI applications create significant barriers for
  visually impaired users undertaking everyday tasks such as recognising local currency
  (Naira).

  6. Educators and parents raised concerns that educational technologies remain poorly
  adapted for children with intellectual and developmental disabilities in Nigeria.

  7. Participants called for the inclusion of persons with disabilities from design
  through to deployment and evaluation of all AI-enabled technologies, not as an afterthought
  but as co-designers.

  8. Participants identified the absence of current policies protecting persons with
  disabilities in AI-enabled environments as a critical gap, and called on government
  to build infrastructure for AI inclusion and develop disability-responsive AI policy.

  '

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'This public engagement project explored
  how persons with disabilities in Nigeria currently experience AI systems, the barriers
  they face, and their recommendations for more inclusive AI design and governance.
  Hosted by the Nigerian AI Research Lab in collaboration with the Centre for Disability
  Inclusion Africa, the engagement took the form of a multi-stakeholder focus group
  discussion held on 4 March 2026 at Arthur Mbanefo Hall, University of Lagos. Thirty-three
  participants representing seven disability clusters, including visual impairment,
  hearing and speech impairment, mobility impairment, intellectual disabilities, dwarfism,
  and albinism, engaged alongside caregivers, AI researchers, technology developers,
  and disability advocates. The discussion was structured around 13 guided questions
  and produced findings and practical recommendations directed at developers, researchers,
  and policymakers.

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'This project merits inclusion for
  several reasons. First, it centres a community that is almost entirely absent from
  AI governance conversations: persons with disabilities in Nigeria and across Africa
  more broadly. The seven disability clusters represented in the discussion reflect
  a breadth of lived experience rarely captured in AI public engagement, spanning
  visual, hearing, speech, mobility, intellectual, and other impairments, as well
  as conditions such as albinism and dwarfism.

  Second, the project demonstrates meaningful inclusion in practice. By purposively
  recruiting across disability type, age, education, and occupation, the engagement
  avoided the tendency to treat disability as a monolithic category. The co-creation
  and interactive format gave participants genuine space to define problems and propose
  solutions on their own terms, rather than simply responding to a predetermined agenda.

  Third, the findings speak directly to governance gaps that are often overlooked
  in dominant AI policy discourse: the absence of disability-responsive data collection,
  the high cost of assistive technologies in low-income contexts, the failure of AI
  systems to account for infrastructure conditions in Nigeria, and the lack of legal
  protections for persons with disabilities in AI-enabled environments. These are
  concerns that neither Western-led AI ethics frameworks nor most African AI policy
  processes have adequately addressed.'

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: 'The most significant inclusion effort was the deliberate recruitment
  across all seven disability clusters, ensuring that groups often marginalised even
  within disability advocacy spaces, such as persons with intellectual disabilities,
  dwarfism, and albinism, had a seat at the table alongside more commonly represented
  groups such as visual and hearing impairment communities. Participants were drawn
  from diverse professional and social backgrounds including students, educators,
  business owners, and caregivers, preventing the discussion from being dominated
  by any single experience of disability. The co-creative and interactive format of
  the focus group was chosen specifically to give participants agency in shaping the
  conversation rather than simply responding to a fixed script. The venue at Arthur
  Mbanefo Hall, University of Lagos was selected to be physically accessible to participants

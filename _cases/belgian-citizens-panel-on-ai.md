# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The Belgian Citizens Panel was composed\
  \ of 60 randomly selected citizens invited to take part in three weekends of learning\
  \ and deliberation in the first part of 2024 related to the evolution of artificial\
  \ intelligence in Europe.\n\nThe Panel was organized as part of the Belgian presidency\
  \ of the European Union, and was the first time a citizens assembly has been organized\
  \ in support of an EU presidency. \n\nOver three weekends between February and April\
  \ 2024, participants came together and took part in multi-lingual deliberations,\
  \ reflecting that Dutch and French are both official languages in Belgium. \n\n\
  Following deliberation, the Panel's findings were shared at a launch event in Brussels.\
  \ Over a three-hour session, members of the citizens panel presented their findings,\
  \ and then took part in a series of discussions asking questions of invited guests,\
  \ including members of the European Parliament, industry representatives, representatives\
  \ of the Belgian EU Presidency and the Belgian Minister of the Interior, International\
  \ Reform of Democratic Renewal.\n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "The project report notes a number of inclusion measures.\n\n\"\
  To eliminate as many barriers as possible, the following\nmeasures were taken:\n\
  \n1\\. Each participant received a fee of 320 euros (the fee was paid after participation\
  \ in the full citizen panel).\n\n2\\. Free lunch, drinks and snacks were provided\
  \ during the citizen panel.\n\n3\\. The participants\u2019 dietary preferences were\
  \ actively taken into account (vegetarian, vegan, halal, etc.)\n\n4\\. For parents\
  \ with children under 12, professional child care was arranged.\n\n5\\. Any individuals\
  \ requiring assistance and citizens who so wished, were allowed to bring a companion.\
  \ The companion could also enjoy the catering provided but did not participate in\
  \ the citizen panel.\n\n6\\. Participants younger than 18 were given permission\
  \ to bring a parent/guardian.\n\n7\\. Participants who lived more than 100 km from\
  \ Brussels were offered hotel stays and dinner each weekend, with guidance.\n\n\
  8\\. First meeting in person: The organising team notified each citizen who had\
  \ been drawn, by phone.\n\n9, Four online information sessions were organised in\
  \ two languages prior to the citizen panel.\n\n10\\. Before and during the citizen\
  \ panel, a contact point was set up where citizens could ask questions at any time\
  \ via phone, email, or WhatsApp.\n\n11\\. All the facilitators were experienced\
  \ in working with a highly diverse target group, and made sure all citizens were\
  \ well looked after, during the sessions and during the breaks.\n\n12\\. Each citizen\
  \ could express him or herself in their own language throughout the process.\n\n\
  13\\. A quiet room was provided for citizens who felt the need to be alone for a\
  \ while.\n\nThe organisers report that these measures \"resulted in 100% participation\
  \ of the 60 citizens at the start of the citizen panel. Only three citizens dropped\
  \ out during the process.\"\n"
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "From Chapter 3 of the Final Report:\n\
  \nAs a team, it was particularly interesting for us to organise a citizen panel\
  \ on such a wide-ranging topic which is so hotly debated and currently so poorly\
  \ understood. As such, we set out several methodological principles to ensure that\
  \ the contribution of the citizen panel would be as relevant as possible to the\
  \ current debate on AI and its regulation: \n\nIt was suggested that the citizens\
  \ could discuss how AI might develop in the EU, rather than give recommendations\
  \ on what the EU or other actors should or should not do. Knowledge of and advances\
  \ in AI are developing at lightning speed, and we wanted to avoid the citizen panel\
  \ focusing on certain technical aspects or elements that could quickly become obsolete.\
  \ The views of the panel are a message to society, not just political players; \n\
  \nThe panel started with a number of experiments with AI, through hands-on demonstrations.\
  \ This gave the citizens the opportunity to talk to the individuals carrying out\
  \ the demonstrations, whereby they all had a similar level of understanding of the\
  \ concrete implications of this technology; \n\nThe citizens themselves determined\
  \ which direction the panel should take. The starting point of the discussions was\
  \ what citizens thought or felt when AI was brought up, rather than a strict presentation\
  \ of what AI is and what the current challenges are. Such a presentation would de\
  \ facto have established a \u201Cframework\u201D for the panel; \n\nThe guest speakers,\
  \ from academia, business and government, responded to the points raised by the\
  \ citizens, based on what the citizens had to say. As such, it was the citizens\
  \ who determined which themes and pillars of AI would be discussed during the 3\
  \ weekends; \n\nGiven that AI is a complex and broad-ranging topic whose ins and\
  \ outs are not yet very clear, we chose to involve a large number of stakeholders\
  \ throughout the process. That way, the citizens could hear different viewpoints\
  \ or perspectives and also adapt their own views based on what they had heard; \n\
  \nThe technological approach (description of how AI and its components work) was\
  \ deliberately excluded from the scope of the discussions: the expertise of the\
  \ citizens involved in a panel like this one is social and human, not technological.\
  \ These carefully-considered choices were discussed with the Oversight Committee,\
  \ which assisted us throughout the process. These exchanges allowed us to adjust\
  \ the process from both a methodological and political perspective, with also the\
  \ preparation of the distribution of the panel\u2019s final report.\n"

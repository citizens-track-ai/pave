# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "In 2025 at team at Carnegie Mellon University\
  \ initiated a process using an online form of community-based participatory research\
  \ to engage approximately 31 people in collaborative design sessions across six\
  \ Discord servers. Participants were involved in collaborative design and shaping\
  \ of the behavior of LLM-powered community bots, with the goal building greater\
  \ community power with respect to shaping AI tools used in their online spaces.\n\
  \nThe project team describe: \n> AI agents, or bots, serve important roles in online\
  \ communities. However, they are often designed by outsiders or a few tech-savvy\
  \ members, leading to bots that may not align with the broader community\u2019s\
  \ needs. How might communities collectively shape the behavior of community bots?\
  \ \n> \n> Botender is a system that enables communities to collaboratively design\
  \ LLM-powered bots without coding. With Botender, community members can directly\
  \ propose, iterate on, and deploy custom bot behaviors tailored to community needs.\
  \ Botender facilitates testing and iteration on bot behavior through case-based\
  \ provocations: interaction scenarios generated to spark user reflection and discussion\
  \ around desirable bot behavior.\n> \n> A validation study found these provocations\
  \ more useful than standard test cases for revealing improvement opportunities and\
  \ surfacing disagreements. \n> \n> During a five-day deployment across six Discord\
  \ servers, Botender supported communities in tailoring bot behavior to their specific\
  \ needs, showcasing the usefulness of case-based provocations in facilitating collaborative\
  \ bot design.\n"

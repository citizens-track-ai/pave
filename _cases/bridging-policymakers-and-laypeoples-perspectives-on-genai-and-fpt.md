# Headline findings
headline-findings: 'We found that policymakers and their advisors generally perceived
  citizen participation as valuable. The main arguments included citizen participation
  to strengthen democratic institutions (84%), facilitate public learning (80%), contribute
  a diversity of views (80%), and include public opinion and judgment they can learn
  from (70%). They expressed interest in citizens'' input on the ethical issues, risks,
  and benefits of genAI and FPT. They focused on specific concerns and trade-offs,
  such as security and privacy, and regulatory measures, such as transparency and
  data protection, as well as general attitudes toward FPT and the acceptability of
  specific use cases of both technologies.


  We found the public to indicate low exposure ratings for FPT in public places and
  high usage and exposure ratings for (text-to-text) genAI systems. This indicates
  a gap between the visibility of the two technologies for laypeople in their everyday
  lives. While FPT systems seem to enter laypeople''s lives rather undetected, genAI
  systems are more visible (in their outputs and the possibility of use) and offer
  opportunities for active engagement. Nigerian, Indian, and South African respondents
  reported the highest use of text-generation AI systems, while those from the UK
  and Canada reported the lowest. Nigerian and South African respondents reported
  the highest awareness of and exposure to FPT technology.


  Our analyses also show that the public perceived genAI to be significantly more
  beneficial than FPT, for which they were undecided. Overall, the majority of respondents
  indicated that they support regulation rather than an outright ban on AI technologies,
  with significantly more support for regulation from respondents in the FPT than
  genAI context. Nigerian respondents showed significantly more rejection of a ban
  on genAI and FPT, and perceived genAI and FPT to be particularly valuable to them
  and to society than participants from other countries. These findings align with
  the positive views of Nigerian respondents throughout the survey and their high
  reported exposure ratings. Brazilian, South Korean, and Canadian participants were
  among the most critical of the presented genAI use cases, and Canadian participants
  were the most critical of several presented FPT use cases.


  The public had the most trust in independent expert-run institutions to establish
  safety measures, and they believed that the government (for FPT) and companies developing
  genAI and FPT should be held accountable in case of harmful outcomes. Here, we did
  not identify any significant differences between respondents from different countries.


  Overall, we observed that the topics suggested by both surveyed groups (the general
  public and policymakers and advisors in AI) largely overlap. Topics raised by laypeople
  are less specific and focus more on leisure-related areas, such as genAI issues
  in the arts and FPT in private use cases or at the airport. Policymakers and advisors
  partially focused on specialized approaches, such as watermarks or AI disclosure
  mechanisms.

  '

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "We are witnessing a rapid global roll-out\
  \ of generative AI (genAI) and facial processing technologies (FPT). How these technologies\
  \ impact societies also depends on local-specific factors that are rarely investigated\
  \ or considered in their development or roll-out. Various institutions and governments\
  \ are actively developing new regulations. In light of these developments, this\
  \ project aims to bridge perspectives between policymakers and laypeople. We conducted\
  \ two questionnaire studies on the topics of generative AI (genAI) and facial processing\
  \ technologies (FPT): one with policymakers and advisors (N = 61) and another with\
  \ laypeople (N = 1070) of twelve nationalities, including Nigeria, South Africa,\
  \ India, Japan, South Korea, Germany, the UK, the USA, Canada, Chile, Mexico, and\
  \ Brazil. Using qualitative and quantitative analyses, our research explores (1)\
  \ the topics related to genAI and FPT that policymakers seek laypeople\u2019s opinions\
  \ on, (2) laypeople\u2019s perceptions of these technologies, and (3) comparisons\
  \ of identified topics to highlight relevant issues for public discussion. We find\
  \ that both AI technologies are adopted differently by laypeople respondents from\
  \ different countries. The topics on which policymakers want to hear the opinions\
  \ of laypeople overlap largely with laypeople\u2019s concerns and topics they deem\
  \ necessary to be further discussed. However, they differ in the level of technological\
  \ depth. Overall, most respondents in both surveyed groups support regulation rather\
  \ than an outright ban on AI technologies. This highlights the need for in-depth\
  \ discursive formats to inform those regulatory efforts, but also to question prevailing\
  \ narratives around both technologies\u2019 capacities.\n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This research represents a comprehensive\
  \ effort to bridge perspectives between policymakers and laypeople by surveying\
  \ topics related to genAI and FPT that policymakers seek laypeople\u2019s opinions\
  \ on (N = 61, 37 nationalities) and mapping them onto citizens' opinions in 12 countries\
  \ (N = 1070). In contrast to many survey studies, in our citizens\u2019 opinion\
  \ survey, we provided information on genAI and FPT before participants responded\
  \ to questions about their deployment. This research built the basis for our follow-up\
  \ project, the Global AI Dialogues. The Global AI Dialogues were informed by the\
  \ results of this study on several accounts: i.a., to narrow down the selection\
  \ of countries, to select the different application contexts of both AI technologies\
  \ that were presented to the participants of the dialogues for discussion, and to\
  \ address country-specifics when applicable."

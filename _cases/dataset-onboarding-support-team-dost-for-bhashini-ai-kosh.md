# Headline findings
headline-findings: "Government departments, universities, civil society organizations,\
  \ and researchers consistently reported that they possessed valuable multilingual\
  \ language resources but lacked awareness of how these resources could contribute\
  \ to AI, as well as a clear pathway to prepare and share them for AI use. \n\nParticipants\
  \ identified technical support, including AI-readiness assessment, metadata creation,\
  \ documentation, licensing, anonymization, and dataset preparation as a greater\
  \ barrier to participation than the availability of language data itself. \n\nContributors\
  \ emphasized that meaningful participation in the AI ecosystem requires more than\
  \ funding, highlighting the importance of long-term partnerships, technical mentorship,\
  \ ecosystem networking, contributor attribution, and clear governance around ownership\
  \ and consent. \n\nOrganizations working with underrepresented Indian languages\
  \ viewed multilingual dataset stewardship as an opportunity to improve representation\
  \ in AI systems, while also expressing a strong need for sustained capacity building\
  \ to transform existing resources such as archives, reports, educational materials,\
  \ and public documents into AI-ready datasets. \n"

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The Dataset Onboarding Support Team (DOST)\
  \ is an initiative led by CivicDataLab, in partnership with the Gates Foundation\
  \ and in collaboration with BHASHINI, to strengthen India's multilingual AI ecosystem\
  \ by enabling organizations and researchers to identify, prepare, and contribute\
  \ AI-ready language datasets to AIKosh, India's national AI repository. \n\nRather\
  \ than collecting new datasets directly, DOST works with government departments,\
  \ universities, civil society organizations, cultural institutions, researchers,\
  \ and community contributors to establish long-term data streams, build capacity\
  \ for AI-ready dataset preparation, and support responsible, contributor-led sharing\
  \ of multilingual language resources. By strengthening the public data infrastructure\
  \ that underpins AI development, DOST aims to ensure that India's linguistic diversity\
  \ is better represented in future AI systems.\n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'DOST demonstrates that participation
  in AI extends beyond consultation on AI policies or systems. It also includes enabling
  communities and institutions to shape the datasets on which AI is built. The project
  brings together government agencies, academic institutions, civil society organizations,
  cultural archives, independent researchers, and language communities to collaboratively
  identify, prepare, and steward multilingual datasets for public benefit. Through
  capacity building, technical assistance, contributor consent, attribution, and long-term
  partnerships, DOST has identified over 120 language datasets across more than 50
  organizations and researchers. The project offers a replicable model for participatory
  data stewardship that strengthens Digital Public Infrastructure while ensuring that
  underrepresented languages and communities have a voice in the future development
  of AI. '

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: 'The project intentionally prioritized participation from organizations
  and communities working with low-resource and underrepresented Indian languages.
  Recruitment combined national outreach with state-level ecosystem mapping and field
  visits to identify contributors beyond established AI networks. Engagement was designed
  to accommodate varying levels of technical capacity by providing hands-on support
  through the DOST Helpdesk, including assistance with AI-readiness assessment, metadata
  creation, documentation, licensing, and dataset preparation. Contributors could
  choose whether to upload datasets themselves or receive support from CivicDataLab,
  while retaining ownership, attribution, and control over access permissions. This
  flexible, contributor centric approach lowered barriers to participation and enabled
  a broader range of institutions, including government departments, universities,
  civil society organizations, cultural archives, and independent researchers to contribute
  to India''s multilingual AI ecosystem.

  '
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "The project operates within a multilingual\
  \ and institutionally diverse ecosystem where datasets vary significantly in quality,\
  \ documentation, licensing, and digital readiness. Many valuable language resources\
  \ remain undigitized, fragmented across organizations, or require substantial preparation\
  \ before they can support AI applications. Building trust with contributors, clarifying\
  \ ownership and licensing, and establishing long-term institutional partnerships\
  \ requires sustained engagement beyond typical project timelines. Additionally,\
  \ low-resource languages often have limited existing digital infrastructure and\
  \ technical expertise, increasing the time and effort required for responsible dataset\
  \ preparation and onboarding. \n"

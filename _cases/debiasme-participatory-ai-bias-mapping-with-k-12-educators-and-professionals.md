# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'DeBiasMe is a community-engaged AI literacy
  initiative that turns AI bias into learning contents publics can explore and act
  on together rather than a technical problem left to experts. Using an AI literacy
  toolkit of tangible "bias mapping" cards and cubes organised around four stages
  of the AI life cycle (prompt/input, data, model training, and output interpretation),
  DeBiasMe runs participatory workshops in which learners, educators or facilitators,
  and AI systems jointly examine where bias enters AI and who is responsible for addressing
  it. Run with K-12 teacher and professional cohorts in South Korea, with ongoing
  parallel pilots in the UK, the workshops surface how non-technical publics perceive
  and regulate AI bias and build their capacity to engage critically with it. The
  initiative connects grassroots engagement to a global standard: DeBiasMe supports
  the South Korea validation of the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students.

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "DeBiasMe brings into the casebook\
  \ a set of voices and methods that are largely absent from the existing evidence\
  \ base:\n1. Communities rarely heard in AI governance: Participants are classroom\
  \ teachers and non-technical professionals, people usually positioned only as end-users\
  \ of AI. \n2. A novel, reusable participation method: The bias-mapping card and\
  \ cube system is a low-tech, hands-on artifact that externalises the AI life cycle\
  \ and makes an abstract concept (\"bias\") concrete, locatable, and negotiable across\
  \ a group. It is designed to be reused by other facilitators.\n3. Our project reframes\
  \ the unit of engagement: Rather than asking individuals to detect bias, the workshop\
  \ distributes the work of scrutinising AI across people, making bias a collective\
  \ and deliberative learning object. \n4. It builds power, not just data. The aim\
  \ is to leave participants more able to question and shape AI in their own settings,\
  \ and to connect that capacity to a global policy instrument (UNESCO AI CFS)."

# Headline findings
headline-findings: 'Across the three cases, several transferable practices emerged:
  (1) treating AI systems as evolving collaborators rather than fixed tools; (2) grounding
  transparency in culturally meaningful metaphors; (3) prioritising participatory
  processual representations over static outputs; and (4) embedding learning and capacity-building
  as core design outcomes. These practices provide actionable guidance for researchers
  and practitioners seeking to operationalise responsible and participatory AI in
  other Indigenous contexts.

  The paper has demonstrated how RAI can be enacted through participatory, community-centred
  design with Indigenous communities in Namibia. By grounding AI development in local
  knowledge systems, cultural values, and long-term relationships, the three cases
  illustrate how global ethical principles can be translated into situated practices
  and lifeworlds. Rather than offering a universal framework, this work contributes
  empirical insights, design practices, and relational methods that support culturally
  grounded, ethical, and resilient AI futures.

  '

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "**Led by academic researchers in collaboration\
  \ with local elders, this 2024 initiative engaged at least 30 members of Namibia's\
  \ Ovahimba and San communities through co-creation workshops and prototyping. By\
  \ centering local cultural values and Indigenous Knowledge Systems, the project\
  \ aimed to establish trustworthy, context-specific AI tools while developing reusable\
  \ participatory methodologies.**\n\nThis research investigates participatory and\
  \ responsible Artificial Intelligence (RAI) practices within three interconnected\
  \ AI-driven projects focused on Namibia\u2019s Indigenous communities, specifically\
  \ the Ovahimba and San. Three AI-driven projects; Himbaverse, Smart Hydroponics,\
  \ and Resilience App, were explored and implemented through a co-design process.\
  \ \n\nThe projects aim to promote responsible AI development that enhance trust\
  \ and aligns with local community aspirations, needs and goals of sustainability\
  \ and integration of Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS) with advanced technology\
  \ to empower local cultures. The research is based on participatory design processes\
  \ and technology prototyping with Ovahimba and San communities in Namibia. It shows\
  \ that transparency and active participation empower communities to contribute to\
  \ development of evolving knowledge bases and system capabilities of RAI. \n\nPreliminary\
  \ findings reveal that responsible AI design must be deeply contextual and shaped\
  \ by local cultural values, knowledge systems, and community-defined objectives.\
  \ The study contributes to the growing discourse on participatory AI development,\
  \ demonstrating how culturally responsive and responsible AI systems can empower\
  \ Indigenous communities and promote equitable technological integration.\n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "In each community, the processes were led by a community elder,\
  \ who recruited m/f members of the youth and community to join on a voluntary basis.\
  \ Approximately 10-20 participants actively participated in each session. The first\
  \ field trips explored aspects of trust, cultural validity and relatability in the\
  \ two sites, as well as providing understandings of what responsible AI, algorithms,\
  \ and automated systems might mean in each of these contexts. Based on ongoing research\
  \ collaboration and ethical protocols between NUST and the two sites, including\
  \ set compensations for translation, coordination, and engagement, were adhered\
  \ to. \n"

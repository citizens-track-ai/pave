# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The name Favel IA is a reference to the\
  \ favelas (slums or shantytowns) themselves as forms of intelligence and to intelig\xEA\
  ncia artificial, artificial intelligence in Portuguese. I\xC1 is also a common ending\
  \ to many samba refrains (la i\xE1 la i\xE1), evoking the idea that favela residents\
  \ must \u201Cdance to the music\u201D of AI systems designed by powerful private\
  \ companies.\n\nFavel IA employs participatory action research (PAR) as its main\
  \ methodological approach, inspired by Latin American scholars such as Orlando Fals\
  \ Borda (1987) and Paulo Freire (2002). The project involves three main groups:\
  \ postgraduate students, favela media activists, and community leaders. The research\
  \ team engaged participants in five consecutive days of workshops. The project asked\
  \ them to document their daily encounters with AI, to carry out empathy exercises\
  \ and to reflect on critical questions about AI and data from a favela standpoint.\
  \ Collectively, the project analysed existing and emerging AI regulation in Brazil\
  \ and elsewhere and co-devised a conceptual note for policymakers (see the report\
  \ attached), articulating the types of AI favela residents desire and the alternative\
  \ futures that they envision. \n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "Favel IA is one of the first projects\
  \ in the world to: 1) focus on AI uses in the daily lives of shantytown communities\
  \ in the majority world; 2) embrace a South-North epistemological and methodological\
  \ flow rather than simply applying Global North and Eurocentric perspectives to\
  \ a \u201Ctest\u201D case study in a majority world context; 3) propose a nuanced\
  \ understanding of the favela itself as a form of intelligence, with the prevalence\
  \ of technologies of care and solidarity; 4) go beyond simplistic understandings\
  \ of tech use in marginalised  communities that revolve solely around the digital\
  \ divide, considering people\u2019s everyday agency and creativity. Additionally,\
  \ the project challenges Euro centric approaches to participation which are offen\
  \ techno-solutionist and instrumentalist in nature. "

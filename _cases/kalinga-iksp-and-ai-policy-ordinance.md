# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'Beginning in 2026, the NYU Peace Research
  and Education Program (PREP) and Kalinga State University worked with community
  in Kalinga to run a Digital Arang to involve approximately 200 people in community-led
  governance discussions to build community power and inform policy-making with respect
  to AI.


  In Kalinga, in the Cordillera highlands of the northern Philippines, an arang is
  the open ground where the community gathers to decide things together. The Digital
  Arang borrowed that gathering and asked a new question: who decides how data and
  AI may touch Indigenous knowledge, and on what terms? Working with Kalinga State
  University, the Indigenous Peoples'' Mandatory Representation, the National Commission
  on Indigenous Peoples, and community elders, the project ran a series of governance
  workshops that produced an Indigenous Knowledge, Systems and Practices (IKSP) framework
  and a local AI Policy Ordinance. The ordinance translates the principle of free,
  prior and informed consent into concrete rules for how Indigenous data may be collected,
  stored, accessed, and refused. It treats opacity, the community''s right not to
  be made legible, as a governance choice rather than a gap to be filled.


  Most participatory AI work in this collection comes from North America and Europe
  and asks how to consult citizens about AI systems built elsewhere. This case starts
  from a different place. It centres an Indigenous community setting binding terms
  on whether and how AI may engage their knowledge at all. The contribution is a working
  model for data and AI governance grounded in Indigenous self-determination and FPIC,
  developed in the community''s own deliberative form rather than imported from a
  citizens-assembly template. It engages a community almost entirely absent from global
  AI governance and shows that the right to refuse is itself a governable principle.

  '

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "The project used the community's own deliberative form, rather\
  \ than imposing an external workshop template, so that authority and pacing stayed\
  \ with the elders. \n\nRecruitment ran through Indigenous representative structures\
  \ (IPMR, NCIP) rather than individual sign-up. \n\nResponsible-AI training was provided\
  \ first so participants engaged the governance questions from an informed position\
  \ rather than being asked to react cold.\n"

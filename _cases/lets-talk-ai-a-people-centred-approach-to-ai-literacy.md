# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "**Let\u2019s Talk AI is a first-of-its-kind\
  \ AI literacy campaign. It\u2019s designed to bring conversations about artificial\
  \ intelligence out of tech labs and boardrooms and move them onto our streets and\
  \ into our homes. Through relatable cartoons and community stories, we\u2019re breaking\
  \ down the basics of AI in everyday life \u2014 whoever you are.**\n\nFrom bus stops\
  \ and community libraries, to radio waves and social media feeds, we made extensive\
  \ efforts to appear across the communities we piloted in, to attempt to include\
  \ as many people as possible. These comics are designed as a pilot for a national\
  \ AI literacy campaign to encourage everyone to feel they can join conversations\
  \ around AI in their lives. Three comics exist as the first phase, enabling people\
  \ to reflect on how AI is changing people\u2019s work and leisure, how we learn\
  \ and how we look at the news and media.\n\nWe are inspired by a few things:\n\n\
  - Dr Susan Oman\u2019s research \u2013 especially on the [Public Voices in AI project](https://digitalgood.net/dg-outputs/peoples-feelings-about-ai/)\n\
  - A wider movement to include all citizens in AI decision-making\n- The belief that\
  \ people deserve better understanding of AI in an AI-driven society\n- The artwork\
  \ of webtoon artist, Kitty McEwan\n- The passion of Margaret Colling, a retired\
  \ librarian from Morecambe Bay \n- The communities and experts we\u2019ve spoken\
  \ to along the way"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "It is the first, as far as we know,\
  \ AI Awareness campaign that has been devised by 'the people', then designed with\
  \ 'the people' in order to create AI literacy that serves 'the people'. \nIt directly\
  \ responds to a prior call to action from participants: taking public voice seriously\
  \ to change practice with a view to impact.\nThe innovative webtoon format has not\
  \ been used in research before, and it is the first national AI literacy campaign\
  \ to utilise a combination of broadcast and paid online and offline marketing to\
  \ raise awareness.\nThe project has been on BBC news online, the BBC came to film\
  \ some of the team. We've been on local radio, in parliament and in the Guardian.\
  \ \nThe project was chosen as part of the official UK delegation to the India AI\
  \ Impact summit, organised by the British Council and the UK's Foreign office. Deputy\

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: '**Inclusion was prioritised across four dimensions:**


  **Representation**: Diverse characters in the comics was an attempt to enable a
  broad range of people to identify with them.


  **Geography**: Workshops took place in the three locations of the three core project
  team members: Susan (academic / lead, Sheffield); Margaret (public participant driving
  the project, Morecambe Bay and Lancaster); Kitty McEwan (artist / co-writer, Plymouth).


  **Recruitment**: Snowball recruitment via community practitioners / organisations
  / infrastructure in the three locations, based on our networks.


  **Accessibility**: Accessibility checks of materials in workshops and final products.'
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "**Resources**: \n\nFollowing action\
  \ points from non-experts enables us to envisage doing things differently. It is\
  \ a very powerful tool. However, it is very difficult in university, policy and\
  \ research funding structures to find a way to resource exploratory work of this\
  \ nature. Paying for offline marketing to raise awareness of the campaign in inclusive\
  \ ways was the majority of the budget.  Paying artists fairly and participants fairly\
  \ took the rest. \n\nThis project was delivered through many gestures of good will\
  \ by everyone else and this points to resource issues around doing things differently\
  \ and doing them properly. The academic labour was unfunded and not resourced. This\
  \ is not possible for most academics and should be recognised. \n\n**Accessibility:**\n\

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'The MTST (Homeless Workers'' Movement)
  Technology Hub is a community-led initiative created within one of Brazil''s largest
  social movements. Established during the COVID-19 pandemic, the project seeks to
  connect struggles for housing, social justice, and digital sovereignty.


  The initiative develops technological capacities among working-class and peripheral
  communities through training programmes in programming, microelectronics, digital
  literacy, and critical technology education. Beyond technical skills, participants
  are encouraged to critically engage with digital infrastructures, algorithms, data
  governance, and the social impacts of emerging technologies.


  The project is organised around three pillars: meaningful community connectivity,
  critical and peripheral technology education, and the creation of dignified alternatives
  to precarious forms of digital labour. It also experiments with participatory technologies,
  civic engagement tools, and community-based approaches to data and digital infrastructures.

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'This project offers a unique example
  of public engagement with technology and AI from the perspective of communities
  that are often excluded from discussions about digital governance. Rather than engaging
  citizens only as consultees, the MTST Technology Hub seeks to build long-term technological
  autonomy and democratic participation among marginalised populations.


  The initiative is particularly significant because it links questions of AI, data
  governance, and digital infrastructures to broader struggles for social justice,
  housing, and economic rights. It demonstrates how communities can move beyond being
  passive users of technology and become active participants in shaping digital futures.


  The project also contributes an important Majority World perspective on public participation,
  highlighting how engagement with AI and technology governance can emerge from grassroots
  social movements instead of formal institutional processes. Its concept of "Popular
  Digital Sovereignty" offers an innovative framework for thinking about democratic
  participation in technology governance.'

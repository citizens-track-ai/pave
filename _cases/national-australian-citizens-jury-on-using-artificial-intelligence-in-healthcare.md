# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "Between 2022 and 2023, the Australian\
  \ Centre for Health Engagement, Evidence and Values at the University of Wollongong\
  \ initiated a process that used a citizens' jury to involve approximately 30 people\
  \ in a national deliberative process to capture qualitative evidence on public attitudes\
  \ to AI in healthcare with the intended outcome of informing the ethical, legal,\
  \ and social implications of adopting machine learning systems for diagnosis and\
  \ screening.\n\nThis citizens\u2019 jury was part of a multi-method bioethics project\
  \ The Algorithm Will See You Now: The Ethical, Legal and Social Implications of\
  \ Adopting Machine Learning Systems for Diagnosis and Screening, which was funded\
  \ by the National Health and Medical Research Council (Ideas Grant 1181960). The\
  \ project was approved by the University of Wollongong Human Research Ethics Committee\
  \ (2022/314). Thirty Australians were randomly selected to participate, and all\
  \ were passionately engaged with the process. The jurors were diverse and closely\
  \ matched to the Australian population on gender, age, ancestry, level of education,\
  \ urban/regional/rural place of residence, and state or territory of residence.\
  \ \n"

# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "**From the submitter:**\nThe focus\
  \ of the study question was screening and diagnosis, but the jurors expressed final\
  \ recommendations regarding AI in health care generally, although the evidence they\
  \ were provided was more limited. Jurors considered four case studies about how\
  \ AI might be used in medical practice; their judgements may have differed had they\
  \ been presented different cases. The jurors\u2019 recommendations are reported\
  \ verbatim, and reflect the limited time available to prepare their wording.\n**From\
  \ the academic article on the citizens' jury:**\nThe procedure followed six core\
  \ steps for deliberative processes: understanding purpose, relationship building,\
  \ skill development, information inputs, group dialogue and deliberation, group\
  \ decision making, and closing. Some activities focused on process, such as structured\
  \ greeting or reflection activities, learning about cognitive bias, and how to ask\
  \ critical questions. Plenary and small group activities alternated with one another;\
  \ small groups were frequently randomly re-allocated for cross-fertilisation of\
  \ perspectives.\n"

  \ supposed to teach you how to be a good person and how to have good integrity.\
  \ Providing guidelines and resources on AI would be a good idea.\u201D - High School\
  \ Student\n\n\"We are not going to be able to avoid this... the sooner we provide\
  \ training and educate teachers and heads of schools, the better. We need to make\
  \ it clear how ethics and credibility are involved...\" - High School Teacher\n\n\
  The most supported policy proposal amongst both students and teachers was that schools\
  \ should provide guidelines and resources to teach students how to use AI responsibly.\
  \ \n\nStudents particularly don\u2019t just fear cheating, but they fear losing\
  \ the ability to think. Even more so than teachers, students agreed more to statements\
  \ about AI tools misinterpreting user questions, providing incorrect responses,\
  \ increasing the spread of fake news and information, and increased use leading\
  \ to students losing their ability to think critically. \n\nStudents and teachers\
  \ both agreed to corrective penalties over punitive ones when students violate the\
  \ school's AI policies. \n\nA majority of students and teachers also both believed\
  \ that students should be allowed to use AI tools as a resource for school work\
  \ and that AI tools should not be banned in school settings. \n"

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The AI in Education Deliberative Polls,\
  \ spearheaded by the Stanford Deliberative Democracy Lab, engage high school students\
  \ and teachers worldwide in rigorous, informed discussions about the future of artificial\
  \ intelligence in schools. This initiative utilizes the Deliberative Polling methodology,\
  \ in which participants review balanced briefing materials, debate policy proposals\
  \ pertaining to AI in small groups, and hear from plenary experts. By surveying\
  \ participants before and after this deep deliberation, the project captures how\
  \ opinions shift once individuals are thoroughly informed, offering crucial, data-driven\
  \ insights to policymakers. Ultimately, this effort provides a blueprint for classroom\
  \ teachers and school districts looking to responsibly integrate AI while actively\
  \ honoring the voices of those directly impacted in the classroom.\n\nThese polls\
  \ have been conducted across the US and internationally in Greece and Albania. \n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This project directly embodies the\
  \ core mission of the PAVE Case Book: inclusive, methodologically rigorous public\
  \ engagement that gives shape to AI governance from the ground up.\n\nThe students\
  \ whose futures are being shaped by AI were the ones who built this process. This\
  \ initiative was conceived and demanded by high school students who recognized that\
  \ consequential decisions about their own learning environments were being made\
  \ without them, right when schools began to implement (often rushed) AI policies\
  \ in the beginning of 2024. In global AI governance discourse, the generation that\
  \ will most profoundly inherit the long-term consequences of AI deployment is almost\
  \ entirely absent from the table. This project offers a replicable template for\
  \ meaningfully embedding youth as architects of governance processes.\n\nMethodological\
  \ rigor of the deliberation elevates this public engagement. Most public consultation\
  \ on AI captures only the loudest, most polarized voices. Stanford's Deliberative\
  \ Polling\xAE framework measures how opinions shift after participants receive balanced\
  \ briefing materials and engage in structured, expert-informed deliberation; such\
  \ methodology also allowed for increasing understanding about key AI concepts amongst\
  \ participants over the course of deliberation. [Deliberative Polling\xAE framework\
  \ involves a random, representative sample is first polled on the targeted issues.\
  \ After this baseline poll, members of the sample are invited to gather at a single\
  \ place for a weekend in order to discuss the issues. cite: https://deliberation.stanford.edu/what-deliberative-pollingr]\
  \ The use of an AI-assisted online moderation platform demonstrates that this quality\
  \ of informed consensus-building is achievable at international scale without geographic\
  \ barriers. The pre/post survey design gives policymakers empirical evidence of\
  \ what the public actually concludes when genuinely informed. \n\nRunning parallel\
  \ deliberative tracks for students and teachers on identical policy questions directly\
  \ maps where these two communities converge and diverge, producing far more actionable\
  \ guidance than either group could generate alone. Both groups ultimately rejected\
  \ outright bans and punitive measures in favor of clear guidance and responsible\
  \ use frameworks, yet meaningful differences in how each group views the other's\
  \ AI use reveal tensions that any credible governance process must surface and address.\n\
  \nThe gap between global AI governance and the people it governs has never been\
  \ wider. This ongoing series of deliberations is proof it doesn't have to be. "

# Headline findings
headline-findings: "1. AI & the Environment (Young people recognise the economic importance\
  \ of data centres but are deeply concerned about their environmental impact.)\n\
  2. AI Chatbots & Companions\_(They recommend prohibiting AI chatbots from providing\
  \ health advice and requiring continuous safety warnings such as \u201CThis is not\
  \ a real person.\u201D)\n3. AI Misinformation & Radicalisation (The Jury emphasises\
  \ the need for AI literacy, improved watermarking, and requirements for AI tools\
  \ to disclose the\_sources used to generate responses)\n4. AI in Education (They\
  \ propose a publicly owned, ethical, free AI chatbot for students supporting learning\
  \ without replacing critical thinking or acting as a therapist.)\n5. AI & the Future\
  \ of Jobs (They recommend clearer regulation of AI use by employers, required disclosure\
  \ when AI is used in\_recruitment, the right to explanations, and limitations on\
  \ using applicants\u2019 data to train models.)\n6. AI Laws & Regulation (Their\
  \ recommendations include prohibiting invasive age\u2010verification methods, requiring\
  \ disclosure of AI training data, protecting artists from data scraping, and introducing\
  \ independent audits and liability structures for AI companies.)\n"

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The National Youth Council of Ireland\
  \ hosted a series of Citizens\u2019 Youth Juries on AI, involving 48 young people\
  \ from across Ireland. It explored young people\u2019s views on the opportunities\
  \ and risks posed by AI, including its impact on education, jobs, misinformation,\
  \ online safety and the environment. Each Jury engaged in a three\u2010stage process\
  \ of learning, deliberation, and recommendation\u2010forming, supported by external\
  \ experts and briefing papers.\_The report of the juries sets out their recommendations\
  \ for policymakers.\n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "NYCI launched an open call for young people to apply to the citizen\u2019\
  s juries. Applicants were not asked to detail their experience or interest in AI.\
  \ Instead, they were required only to provide basic contact information and demographic\
  \ details through an online form. The form was openly accessible to all young people\
  \ via NYCI\u2019s website. This was complemented by pro\u2011active outreach on\
  \ social media and emails to more than 400 DEIS schools, UBU Youth Services, and\
  \ Comhairle na n\xD3g groups.\n\nUsing data available via the CSO and other sources,\
  \ the project team developed an estimated demographic profile of Irish youth. The\
  \ selection process aimed to ensure each of the following demographics were represented\
  \ on the juries. \n\u2022 Young people of all gender identities.\n\u2022 Young people\
  \ of different ages.\n\u2022 Disabled young people \n\u2022 LGBT+ young people\n\
  \u2022 Young people from a variety of racial \nand ethnic backgrounds\n\u2022 Young\
  \ people living in rural Ireland\n\u2022 Young people living in different \nparts\
  \ of Ireland and within areas \nexperiencing economic disadvantage.\n\u2022 Young\
  \ people who left education \nearly.\nThe estimated demographic  profile informed\
  \ the development of quotas and targets for the recruitment process.\n\nA code developed\
  \ by researchers at the Insight SFI Research Centre for Data Analytics in DCU was\
  \ then used to randomly select 48 jurors across 6 juries while maintaining the desired\
  \ demographic balance laid out in the quotas.\n"

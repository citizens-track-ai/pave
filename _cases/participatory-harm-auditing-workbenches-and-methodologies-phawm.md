# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "Through the novel concept of participatory\
  \ AI auditing, PHAWM empowers a diverse set of stakeholders without a background\
  \ in AI, such as domain experts, end-users, regulators, decision subjects, and communities,\
  \ to undertake audits of AI to identify impacts, both positive and negative. Our\
  \ research is grounded in four use cases: Health, Media Content, Cultural Heritage\
  \ and Collaborative Content Generation. \n\nA significant barrier to fully understanding\
  \ the implications of predictive and generative AI is their unassessed potential\
  \ for impact. Hence, AI auditing has become imperative in responsible AI governance,\
  \ in line with existing and impending regulatory frameworks. Yet, AI auditing has\
  \ been haphazard, unsystematic, and left exclusively in the hands of people with\
  \ technical AI expertise. PHAWM brings together a consortium of 7 academic institutions,\
  \ and over 20 partner organisations in the United Kingdom, to fix this fundamental\
  \ challenge. \n\nTo enable stakeholders to carry out a participatory audit, our\
  \ project has produced an open-access workbench, which support stakeholders in evaluating\
  \ the quality and potential impacts of AI. The participatory audit is embedded in\
  \ our methodology, which guides the underlying principles and how, when and who\
  \ carries out participatory audits. We are also developing training and resources\
  \ to support for assurance and compliance for organisations to develop and deploy\
  \ AI responsibly.\n\nInstead of a focus on technical model performance or optimisation,\
  \ PHAWM promotes a socio-technical approach to evaluate the harms of an AI application\
  \ throughout the AI lifecycle (from ideation to deployment) and to audit the AI\
  \ application's impact in specific context that it is used. In addition, while traditional\
  \ auditing requires access to both training data and model to complete an audit,\
  \ PHAWM\u2019s workbench and methodology can accommodate auditing in contexts where\
  \ the model and/or the training data are not accessible.  \n\nOur research highlights\
  \ that people with lived experience and domain expertise can uncover blind spots\
  \ in AI application outcomes, complementing technical expertise in AI auditing and\
  \ PHAWM\u2019s workbench and methodology, therefore, helps address the urgent need\
  \ for rigorous assessments of AI risks caused by the rapid expansion and adoption\
  \ of the technology across a wide range of sectors.  \n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "PHAWM is leading innovative research\
  \ in methods of AI auditing, promoting participatory audits to be \u2018gold standard\u2019\
  \ to complement traditional AI audits. PHAWM\u2019s approach to participatory audits\
  \ is a 'game changer' in empowering diverse stakeholders and in centering the voices\
  \ of the end-users, people, and communities whose life or working practices are\
  \ impacted by AI, addressing a public accountability gap in current practice. This\
  \ approach helps to actively involve stakeholders who are usually excluded from\
  \ the AI audit process, including those who will be affected by the decisions made\
  \ by an AI application, to produce fairer and more equitable outcomes of AI application.\
  \ \n\nInstead of a focus on technical model performance or optimisation, PHAWM promotes\
  \ a socio-technical approach to evaluate the harms of an AI application throughout\
  \ the AI lifecycle (from ideation to deployment) and to audit the AI application's\
  \ impact in specific context that it is used. In addition, while traditional auditing\
  \ requires access to both training data and model to complete an audit, PHAWM\u2019\
  s workbench and methodology can accommodate auditing in contexts where the model\
  \ and/or the training data are not accessible.  \n\nOur research highlights that\
  \ people with lived experience and domain expertise can uncover blind spots in AI\
  \ application outcomes, complementing technical expertise in AI auditing and ev\
  \ PHAWM\u2019s workbench and methodology, therefore, helps address the urgent need\
  \ for rigorous assessments of AI risks caused by the rapid expansion and adoption\
  \ of the technology across a wide range of sectors.  \n \nThe workbench and its\
  \ accompanying methodology, which guides organisations and communities to use the\
  \ tool effectively, are both publicly available and free to download from the project\u2019\
  s website. "

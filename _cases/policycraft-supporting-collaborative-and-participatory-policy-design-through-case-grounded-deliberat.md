# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "To address the top-down nature of AI\
  \ policy design, researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed and tested\
  \ \"PolicyCraft,\" an online system that scaffolds collaborative policy-making through\
  \ case-grounded deliberation. During a 2024 field study, 68 university students\
  \ used the platform asynchronously to propose, critique, and vote on course policies\
  \ for generative AI, demonstrating how structured, case-based discussion can help\
  \ communities reach greater consensus.\n\nCommunity and organizational AI policies\
  \ are typically designed in a top-down, centralized fashion, with limited input\
  \ from impacted stakeholders. This can result in policies that are misaligned with\
  \ community needs or perceived as illegitimate. How can we support more collaborative,\
  \ participatory approaches to policy design? In this project, we present PolicyCraft,\
  \ a system that structures collaborative policy design through case-grounded deliberation.\
  \ \n\nBuilding on past research that highlights the value of concrete cases in establishing\
  \ common ground, PolicyCraft supports users in collaboratively proposing, critiquing,\
  \ and revising policies through discussion and voting on cases. A field study across\
  \ two university courses showed that students using PolicyCraft reached greater\
  \ consensus and developed better-supported course policies, compared with those\
  \ using a baseline system that did not scaffold their use of concrete cases.\n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This project should be included\
  \ in the case book for the following reasons:\n\n(a) The system introduced in this\
  \ project, called PolicyCraft, is specifically designed to surface diverse viewpoints\
  \ regarding the wording of policies or the fundamental differences in perspectives\
  \ on how specific cases impacted by those policies should be handled. The system\
  \ further facilitates deliberation on these viewpoints to help establish consensus\
  \ based on concrete cases.\n\n(b) The system is intended to empower a bottom-up,\
  \ collaborative approach to policy design, in contrast to the more common top-down,\
  \ centralized methods.\n\n(c) The system introduces the unique mechanism of \"case-grounded\
  \ deliberation\" to support effective policy discussion. The project has been cited\
  \ by the European Parliament's Policy Department for Justice, Civil Liberties, and\
  \ Institutional Affairs in their examination of the use of technology to monitor\
  \ the application of EU law: \n\nhttps://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/STUD/2025/777915/IUST_STU(2025)777915_EN.pdf\n\
  \nOverall, this project presents a system that is highly valuable for collaborative\
  \ approaches to AI governance and to the broader field of public policy design."

# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "The field study is currently being\
  \ conducted at a university, where students collaboratively design course policies\
  \ on the responsible use of AI. Conducting a broader field study in real-world settings\
  \ would be beneficial for further demonstrating the system's effectiveness.\n\n\
  From the author's academic article:\n> PolicyCraft is designed to support policy\
  \ design across a broad range of community contexts. While the current study focuses\
  \ on supporting students in collaboratively designing course policies on the use\
  \ of generative AI, we envision its application in online communities like subreddits\
  \ to develop content moderation policies [[39](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713865#Bib0039)],\
  \ or in local communities such as vTaiwan to refine regulations for emerging technologies\
  \ [[33](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713865#Bib0033)]. Still, some implementations\
  \ of PolicyCraft will require careful adaptation to align with specific communities\u2019\
  \ norms. For example, to encourage policy iteration while holding participants accountable\
  \ for their edits, the current implementation of PolicyCraft adopts Wikipedia\u2019\
  s approach by allowing all participants to edit policies while maintaining a transparent\
  \ edit history [[46](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713865#Bib0046)]. While\
  \ this approach has shown to be effective in supporting coordination and preventing\
  \ vandalism in peer production [[20](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713865#Bib0020),\
  \ [84](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713865#Bib0084)] and the current\
  \ study, a more robust moderation strategy may be required when applying PolicyCraft\
  \ in different contexts. Future research should investigate which aspects of PolicyCraft\u2019\
  s current design need to be adapted for use by other communities and identify which\
  \ findings from the current study are most transferable across diverse contexts.\n\
  \n"

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "In 2022, Carnegie Mellon University co-created\
  \ a process that used community-based participatory research and AI lifecycle comicboarding\
  \ to involve approximately 21 social workers and unhoused people in participatory\
  \ design sessions to evaluate a deployed AI-based decision-support system used in\
  \ homeless services with the intended outcomes of shaping future AI tools, empowering\
  \ impacted communities, and creating re-usable participation methods.\n\nRecent\
  \ years have seen growing adoption of AI-based decision-support systems (ADS) in\
  \ homeless services, yet we know little about stakeholder desires and concerns surrounding\
  \ their use. In this work, we aim to understand impacted stakeholders\u2019 perspectives\
  \ on a deployed ADS that prioritizes scarce housing resources. We employed AI lifecycle\
  \ comicboarding, an adapted version of the comicboarding method, to elicit stakeholder\
  \ feedback and design ideas across various components of an AI system\u2019s design.\
  \ We elicited feedback from county workers who operate the ADS daily, service providers\
  \ whose work is directly impacted by the ADS, and unhoused individuals in the region.\
  \ Our participants shared concerns and design suggestions around the AI system\u2019\
  s overall objective, specific model design choices, dataset selection, and use in\
  \ deployment. Our findings demonstrate that stakeholders, even without AI knowledge,\
  \ can provide specific and critical feedback on an AI system\u2019s design and deployment,\
  \ if empowered to do so.\n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This project should be included\
  \ in the case book for the following reasons:\n\n(a) The feedback elicitation method\
  \ introduced in this project, called AI Lifecycle Comicboarding, is specifically\
  \ designed to gather the unique perspectives of community stakeholders who may not\
  \ have AI literacy, but who have lived experience in the specific context in which\
  \ they live or work.\n\n(b) The project partners with local shelters to engage community\
  \ stakeholders, especially individuals with lived experience of homelessness, to\
  \ gather their critiques and feedback on the AI system used to allocate public housing,\
  \ which directly impacts their lives.\n\n(c) The AI Lifecycle Comicboarding method\
  \ is a novel approach that can effectively solicit targeted feedback at different\
  \ stages of the AI development lifecycle, regardless of the participant\u2019s background.\n\
  \nOverall, this project demonstrates that community stakeholders can provide meaningful\
  \ feedback and critique when appropriately empowered to do so."

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "Throughout the project, we collaborated with professionals who\
  \ are experts in social work and have experience working with individuals with lived\
  \ experience of homelessness. This collaborative effort ensured that our study methods,\
  \ materials, and procedures were understandable, respectful, and engaging for participants\
  \ of all backgrounds and experiences. For example, our comic boards break down the\
  \ AI development lifecycle to elicit specific feedback on different components of\
  \ an AI system\u2019s design. Our comic boards also combine illustrations with brief,\
  \ carefully crafted captions to increase accessibility to individuals with lower\
  \ literacy. Meanwhile, we create a gender-neutral persona that allows participants\
  \ to self-determine when to bring in their own lived experiences and when to distance\
  \ themselves from the discussion subjects at hand. Last but not least, we conduct\
  \ one-on-one sessions with participants, but share de-identified responses from\
  \ prior study participants from other stakeholder groups, to facilitate asynchronous\
  \ inter-group deliberation.\n"
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "From the Authors' reflections in\
  \ their academic article:\n\nIn this study, we used AI lifecycle comicboarding to\
  \ solicit feedback and suggestions for design modifications to an AI system _after_\
  \ that system had already been deployed. While collecting post-deployment feedback\
  \ is critical, as discussed in Section [5.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3580882?__cf_chl_f_tk=Uy7iNwiEvg9nB64Rq.WK8SjfZnw_dURgpz1q_NRDX.8-1783080465-1.0.1.1-WLDayvweruRlEpMF72tM6kMya..XHKVTsDcU8DLlPAQ#sec-5-1),\
  \ we also encourage using it early in the design process of a new AI system (e.g.,\
  \ conceptualization, prototyping, or pilot testing stages). Indeed, at later stages,\
  \ once significant resources have been invested in an AI system\u2019s development,\
  \ there is a risk that system developers will be hesitant to implement broader changes\
  \ such as those discussed in Section [5.2.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3580882?__cf_chl_f_tk=Uy7iNwiEvg9nB64Rq.WK8SjfZnw_dURgpz1q_NRDX.8-1783080465-1.0.1.1-WLDayvweruRlEpMF72tM6kMya..XHKVTsDcU8DLlPAQ#sec-5-2-1)\
  \ and Section [5.3.3](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3580882?__cf_chl_f_tk=Uy7iNwiEvg9nB64Rq.WK8SjfZnw_dURgpz1q_NRDX.8-1783080465-1.0.1.1-WLDayvweruRlEpMF72tM6kMya..XHKVTsDcU8DLlPAQ#sec-5-3-3),\
  \ and may instead be biased towards more incremental changes that can be implemented\
  \ with fewer resources. When using the method before a system is already in place,\
  \ the final panel of each comicboard (e.g., Figure [1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3580882?__cf_chl_f_tk=Uy7iNwiEvg9nB64Rq.WK8SjfZnw_dURgpz1q_NRDX.8-1783080465-1.0.1.1-WLDayvweruRlEpMF72tM6kMya..XHKVTsDcU8DLlPAQ#fig1)\
  \ (e)) can be used to elicit feedback on _proposed_ designs. With this flexibility,\
  \ our method is intended to empower participants not only to critique and redesign\
  \ existing AI systems, but also to redirect the design processes of proposed systems\
  \ at the earliest stages of the design process.\n\nIn the future, more formal and\
  \ controlled evaluations of AI lifecycle comicboarding would help further understand\
  \ its strengths and limitations in elicting specific feedback compared to standard\
  \ interviews or alternative comicboarding approaches. We envision that our method\
  \ could be adapted and used to elicit feedback from other stakeholder groups who\
  \ experience intersectional social disadvantages, such as children and families\
  \ subject to out-of-home placements and state intervention or individuals with intellectual/developmental\
  \ or psychiatric disabilities. In future work, we also plan to develop a suite of\
  \ toolkits based on the comicboards generated for this study, along with instructions\
  \ on how to adapt and use them. We hope that releasing these toolkits will help\
  \ HCI practitioners and developers of AI systems to better integrate the voices\
  \ and perspectives of impacted stakeholders.\n"

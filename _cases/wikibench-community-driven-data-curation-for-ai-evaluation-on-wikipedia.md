# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "In 2023 at team at Carnegie Mellon University\
  \ piloted a new tool that that engaged approximately 17 people in collaborative\
  \ data curation and system evaluation workshops to review and shape the development\
  \ of AI content moderation tools on Wikipedia. The project sought to test methods\
  \ and create re-usable participation that can better enable community-driven AI\
  \ evaluation.\n\n\n> AI tools are increasingly deployed in community contexts. However,\
  \ datasets used to evaluate AI are typically created by developers and annotators\
  \ outside a given community, which can yield misleading conclusions about AI performance.\
  \ How might communities be empowered to drive the intentional design and curation\
  \ of evaluation datasets for AI that impacts them? \n> \n> This was the question\
  \ explored by the Wikibench project, focussing on Wikipedia, an online community\
  \ with multiple AI-based content moderation tools currently deployed. The system\
  \ that enables communities to collaboratively curate AI evaluation datasets, while\
  \ navigating ambiguities and differences in perspective through discussion. \n>\
  \ \n> A field study on Wikipedia shows that datasets curated using Wikibench can\
  \ effectively capture community consensus, disagreement, and uncertainty. Furthermore,\
  \ study participants used Wikibench to shape the overall data curation process,\
  \ including refining label definitions, determining data inclusion criteria, and\
  \ authoring data statements.\n"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'This project should be included
  in the case book for the following reasons:


  (a) The system introduced in the project is specifically designed to reveal potential
  sources of disagreement regarding the labeling of AI datasets and to facilitate
  deliberation on these diverse points of view.


  (b) The system is intended for communities (Wikipedia editors being an example in
  this case) who are significantly impacted by the use of AI but historically have
  not had a say in evaluating the AI systems that greatly impact their knowledge work.


  (c) The system represents the first technical infrastructure that enables communities
  to collectively curate and discuss AI datasets. It also empowers community members
  with the agency to author label definitions, set data inclusion criteria, and draft
  data statements to ensure the responsible use of the dataset.


  Overall, the project demonstrates the concrete benefits of a collaborative, community-driven
  approach to AI data curation, rather than leaving it to outsiders.'

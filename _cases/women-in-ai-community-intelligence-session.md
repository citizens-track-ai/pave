# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'A community intelligence session bringing
  together women from Black and Global Majority communities to document lived experiences
  of AI harm and generate policy recommendations for Parliament. Held on 25 March
  2026 in East London, in partnership with Blacq Space. Five structured discussion
  groups produced potential policy recommendations and eight personal testimonies
  documented real experiences of AI harm including deepfakes, coordinated harassment,
  algorithmic suppression of cultural speech, and identity impersonation. Findings
  directly informed BAIF''s written evidence submission on the Online Safety Act.


  This project engaged a community almost entirely absent from AI governance conversations,
  women from Black and Global Majority backgrounds, and created a direct pipeline
  from lived experience to parliamentary evidence. The testimony gathered is not anecdotal:
  it maps onto specific legislative gaps in the Online Safety Act and is being submitted
  to DSIT and the AI APPG as formal written evidence. The project demonstrates that
  community-rooted intelligence, gathered through deliberate and inclusive methods,
  can produce policy-grade evidence. It also represents a model for how marginalised
  communities can enter AI governance processes on their own terms rather than as
  consultees.

  '

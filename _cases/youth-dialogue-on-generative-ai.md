# Headline findings
headline-findings: "### Use of Generative AI in Education\n\nParticipants in the youth\
  \ dialogue were generally positive about the use of generative AI in education.\
  \ They described the technology as a valuable tool that helps them save time and\
  \ manage the pressures of school life. In particular, they highlighted its usefulness\
  \ for understanding and rephrasing information and for acting as a learning companion\
  \ or brainstorming partner.\n\nAt the same time, many participants expressed concerns\
  \ that AI can become a slippery slope, where support may lead to dependency, reduced\
  \ motivation, and weaker learning outcomes. Some felt that AI is already being used\
  \ in ways that undermine learning and self-discipline.\n\nParticipants also reported\
  \ a lack of clear frameworks for AI use in education. Existing rules were often\
  \ perceived as inconsistent, and many felt they lacked guidance on how to use the\
  \ technology constructively.\n\nOverall, participants stated that generative AI:\n\
  \n- Is a valuable learning tool.\n- Helps reduce time pressure and academic stress.\n\
  - Has important limitations.\n- Can weaken motivation and learning.\n- Requires\
  \ clearer rules, guidance, and expectations for educational use.\n\n\n\n### Experiences\
  \ and Perspectives on Deepfakes\n\nDeepfakes were not considered a major part of\
  \ participants\u2019 daily lives. Those who had encountered them mainly described\
  \ humorous videos featuring politicians or celebrities doing or saying unrealistic\
  \ things. Some participants felt such content was acceptable if the individuals\
  \ involved had consented or if it was clearly identifiable as artificial. Others\
  \ believed the technology should be banned because of its potential risks.\n\nParticipants\
  \ were particularly concerned about the future implications of deepfakes. They worried\
  \ that deepfakes could erode trust in online information, media, and interpersonal\
  \ relationships. Many were concerned about the ability of deepfakes to misrepresent\
  \ people and damage reputations.\n\nA major concern was the potential use of deepfakes\
  \ for revenge, bullying, harassment, and blackmail. Female participants expressed\
  \ greater concern about personal consequences than male participants.\n\nOverall,\
  \ participants stated that:\n\n- Deepfakes are not a major part of their everyday\
  \ lives.\n- The deepfakes they encounter are mostly entertainment-oriented.\n- They\
  \ are concerned that deepfakes could undermine trust in society.\n- They find it\
  \ worrying that deepfakes can be used to misrepresent people.\n- They are particularly\
  \ concerned about their use for revenge, bullying, and blackmail.\n\n\n\n### Use\
  \ of Generative AI for Personal Purposes\n\nParticipants expressed considerable\
  \ skepticism about using generative AI for personal and emotional matters. Most\
  \ actively avoided using AI in contexts related to feelings, personal relationships,\
  \ or private life. This reluctance was driven by concerns about data privacy, the\
  \ lack of human qualities in AI systems, and broader societal implications.\n\n\
  Among those who did use generative AI for personal purposes, common applications\
  \ included job applications, learning about hobbies, cooking advice, and entertainment\
  \ with friends. Only a small number reported using AI for sensitive topics such\
  \ as relationships or pregnancy.\n\nSome participants suggested that AI could potentially\
  \ support people who experience loneliness or social difficulties. However, many\
  \ worried that increased reliance on AI could weaken social skills and human relationships\
  \ over time.\n\nParticipants drew a clear distinction between humans and technology.\
  \ They often described AI-generated relationships as \u201Cfake,\u201D \u201Ccold,\u201D\
  \ and \u201Csuperficial,\u201D arguing that AI lacks the lived experience necessary\
  \ to truly understand, care for, or advise people.\n\nData privacy was another major\
  \ concern, with many participants worried about how personal information shared\
  \ with AI systems might be used or stored.\n\nOverall, participants stated that:\n\
  \n- Around one in five specifically requested education and training on the responsible\
  \ use of generative AI.\n- They generally do not use AI for emotional or private\
  \ matters.\n- They dislike replacing human relationships with artificial technologies.\n\
  - They are concerned that society may become less socially connected in the future.\n\
  - Some use AI for job searches, hobbies, and everyday practical tasks.\n- Very few\
  \ use AI to discuss sensitive personal issues.\n- They are concerned about the misuse\
  \ of personal data."

# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "**To explore the impact of generative\
  \ AI on education, Democracy x engaged 375 students and educational stakeholders\
  \ through classroom-integrated dialogues and co-creation workshops. By embedding\
  \ the participation process directly into school curricula, the project gathered\
  \ diverse youth perspectives to co-create actionable policy recommendations for\
  \ the Danish education sector.**\n\n**Background:**\n\nAs part of the Danish initiative\
  \ Viden & Demokrati (Knowledge & Democracy), a national participation project was\
  \ conducted to explore how young people interact with and are affected by generative\
  \ AI. The project responded to the rapid adoption of AI technologies and the need\
  \ to better understand their impact on education and social relationships. \n\n\
  The project was grounded in principles of participatory technology assessment and\
  \ deliberative democracy, emphasising informed dialogue, collective reflection,\
  \ and the inclusion of citizens in discussions about emerging technologies. The\
  \ overall objective was twofold: to strengthen young people\u2019s understanding\
  \ of generative AI and its ethical implications, and to generate knowledge about\
  \ how AI is shaping their everyday lives. \n\n\n**The Youth Dialogue:**\n\nIn autumn\
  \ 2024, a national youth dialogue engaged 361 students from Danish upper secondary\
  \ and vocational education programmes. The dialogue focused on three themes: \n\n\
  \\- Learning and education  \n\\- Deepfakes and misinformation  \n\\- Personal relationships\
  \ and social interactions  \n\nInspired by the EuropeSay participation method, the\
  \ dialogue was conducted as an online, self-facilitated process integrated into\
  \ regular classroom activities. Participants received accessible background information,\
  \ discussed the topics in small groups, and shared individual reflections and experiences\
  \ through a structured digital platform. \n\nRather than collecting immediate opinions\
  \ through a traditional survey, the method enabled participants to develop informed\
  \ perspectives through dialogue and reflection. This approach supported both democratic\
  \ engagement and learning while generating valuable insights into young people\u2019\
  s experiences with generative AI. \n\n**Key Findings: **\n\nThe dialogue revealed\
  \ that generative AI has become a significant part of many young people\u2019s lives,\
  \ particularly in relation to education. Participants generally viewed AI as a useful\
  \ learning tool that can support schoolwork and help manage time pressure. At the\
  \ same time, they expressed concerns about overreliance on the technology, its limitations,\
  \ and its potential impact on motivation and learning outcomes. \n\nA recurring\
  \ theme was the need for clearer guidance and more open dialogue about appropriate\
  \ AI use in educational settings. While many students were already using generative\
  \ AI, relatively few felt adequately equipped to use it constructively and responsibly.\
  \ \n\n\n**From Dialogue to Action:**\n\nTo ensure that the perspectives collected\
  \ through the youth dialogue led to concrete outcomes, a stakeholder workshop was\
  \ organized on 14 May 2025 at Kolding Library. The workshop brought together 14\
  \ representatives from upper secondary schools and vocational education institutions,\
  \ including students, teachers, and school leaders. \n\nUsing the findings from\
  \ the youth dialogue, participants worked collaboratively to translate young people\u2019\
  s needs and concerns into actionable recommendations for the education sector. \n\
  \nThe workshop resulted in four key recommendations: \n\n\\- Establish clearer national\
  \ frameworks for the use of generative AI in education.  \n\\- Review and update\
  \ existing governance and policy documents.  \n\\- Strengthen local and national\
  \ professional networks among educators.  \n\\- Promote dialogue, experimentation,\
  \ and practical learning about AI in classrooms.  \n\nThe recommendations highlighted\
  \ that addressing generative AI in education requires action at multiple levels,\
  \ from classroom practices to national policy frameworks. \n\n\n**Method and Democratic\
  \ Impact:**\n\nThe project demonstrates how educational settings can be used as\
  \ arenas for democratic participation and knowledge exchange. By embedding participation\
  \ activities into teaching, the project successfully engaged a broad and diverse\
  \ group of young people who are often underrepresented in formal decision-making\
  \ processes. \n\nThe methodology combines information-sharing, facilitated dialogue,\
  \ and structured feedback mechanisms, creating a two-way exchange between knowledge\
  \ institutions and citizens. In this case, the project was developed in collaboration\
  \ with the Danish Data Ethics Council, ensuring that the results could inform ongoing\
  \ discussions related to AI and digitalization. \n\nBeyond generating insights,\
  \ the process strengthened participants\u2019 ability to engage in respectful, reflective,\
  \ and informed discussions about complex societal issues. The project therefore\
  \ contributed not only to understanding young people\u2019s experiences with generative\
  \ AI but also to fostering democratic competencies and civic engagement. \n\n\n\
  **Outcomes:**\n\nThe project serves as a model for how participatory processes can\
  \ connect knowledge, policy, and citizens, ensuring that technological developments\
  \ are informed by the experiences and needs of those most affected by them. \n\n\
  Results were presented at an open event as well as for the Children\u2019s, Education\
  \ and Research Committee"

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'This case is particularly relevant
  because it successfully engaged a large and diverse group of 361 young people, a
  demographic that is often underrepresented in public discussions. Young people are
  moreover a particularly relevant group as they are among those most affected by
  the rapid adoption of generative AI. By partnering with upper secondary schools
  and vocational education institutions, the project reached young people who might
  not otherwise participate in democratic engagement or policy-related processes.


  A distinctive feature of the case is its integration of public participation into
  formal education. Rather than relying on voluntary participation, the dialogue was
  embedded within classroom teaching, making engagement accessible, scalable, and
  inclusive. This approach enabled participants to learn about generative AI, discuss
  its opportunities and risks, and develop informed opinions through structured deliberation.


  As a result, the case offers valuable lessons on how to engage young people in AI
  governance, combine democratic participation with learning, and create meaningful
  pathways from public dialogue to action. These features make it a strong example
  of inclusive and impactful public engagement on AI.'

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: 'The primary inclusion effort focused on engaging young people
  from diverse educational backgrounds, including both upper secondary schools and
  vocational education programmes. This was a deliberate choice to ensure that perspectives
  were gathered from groups with different experiences, educational pathways, and
  relationships to technology.


  To make participation accessible, the dialogue was integrated into regular teaching
  activities and conducted during school hours, reducing barriers to participation.
  The process was adjusted based on dialogue with participating schools and teachers,
  who provided input on how the dialogue format could best fit their educational settings.'
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: 'While the project successfully engaged
  a large number of young people, some limitations should be noted. The self-facilitated
  format worked well in most cases but made it harder for teachers to support discussions
  and maintain engagement in some groups. Younger participants occasionally found
  the questions too abstract or repetitive, which may have limited the depth of responses.


  There were also differences between educational settings, requiring further adaptations
  to language and materials, particularly for vocational education students. Finally,
  participant feedback suggested that the dialogue often reinforced existing views
  on generative AI rather than significantly changing perspectives, and some evaluation
  questions had a relatively high non-response rate.'

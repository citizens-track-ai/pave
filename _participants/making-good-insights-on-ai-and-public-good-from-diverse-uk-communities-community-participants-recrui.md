# Recruitment description
recruitment-description: "Community recruitment, via community research networks and\
  \ place based approaches. Participants were recruited by six community researchers\
  \ working in Belfast, Brixton and Southampton using trusted local networks and place-based\
  \ outreach. Recruitment approaches were tailored to each locality and included voluntary\
  \ sector networks, community events, libraries, caf\xE9s, local organisations and\
  \ social media. Interested participants completed a common expression-of-interest\
  \ form and were selected through a collaborative process between Ada researchers\
  \ and community researchers. Recruitment intentionally sought participants from\
  \ groups frequently underrepresented in AI governance, asking applicants to self-identify\
  \ across a number of dimensions of exclusion (including disability, ethnicity, citizenship,\
  \ poverty, LGBTQIA+ identity and communities not listened to by those in power).\
  \ The project prioritised inclusion and diversity over demographic representativeness."
# Was there a specific target population?
was-there-a-specific-target-population: People from communities whose perspectives
  are often underrepresented in AI research and policy, recruited through trusted
  local community networks. The project sought participants reflecting local diversity
  and experiences of exclusion, including (through self-identification) people who
  had experienced poverty, disability, ethnic minoritisation, migration or citizenship-related
  exclusion, LGBTQIA+ identities, and communities who felt unheard by decision-makers.
  Rather than seeking statistical representativeness, the project aimed for diverse,
  community-rooted perspectives.

# Additional notes
additional-notes: The project adopted a community-led research model in which six
  community researchers worked alongside Ada researchers through co-design, recruitment,
  facilitation and co-analysis. Alongside structured deliberative workshops and learning
  sessions, participants were invited to contribute through a flexible enquiry process
  including diaries, interviews, audio reflections and other creative methods. The
  project therefore combined deliberative engagement with ethnographic and participatory
  research methods to build a richer understanding of how people made sense of AI
  and the public good. Participants also reconvened after analysis to review and challenge
  the emerging findings before publication.

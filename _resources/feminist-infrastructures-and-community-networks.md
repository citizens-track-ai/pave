# Short description
short-description: This resource examines how feminist organisations and community
  groups create, manage, and sustain their own digital infrastructures and communication
  networks. Grounded in principles of care, collective responsibility, accessibility,
  digital security, and social justice, it showcases alternative approaches to technology
  development and governance that emerge from grassroots experiences.  The report
  illustrates how communities can take an active role in shaping technological systems
  by developing their own infrastructures, practices, and governance models. It provides
  valuable insights into participatory and community-led approaches to technology,
  highlighting the importance of autonomy, collective knowledge production, and democratic
  decision-making in digital environments.  This resource is particularly relevant
  to discussions on public engagement in AI and technology governance because it demonstrates
  how communities that are often excluded from decision-making processes can build
  the skills, resources, and organisational capacity needed to influence and shape
  technological futures on their own terms.
